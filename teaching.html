Teaching

Courses

I teach comparative politics, the political economy of gender, and the politics of climate change at both the undergraduate and graduate level. I also serve as Vice-Chair and Director of Undergraduate Studies in the Department of Political Science.

Undergraduate

  • Political Economy of Gender

    How gender shapes political and economic life, and how women’s rights evolve across time and space.

  • Introduction to Comparative Politics

    Why countries are governed so differently, and how political scientists explain it.

  • Women in the Political Pipeline (WIPP) Initiative

    Non-partisan training on how to run for office, through a partnership with Emerge California, paired with research on why women are underrepresented.

Graduate

  • Politics of Climate Change

    How fossil fuels concentrate power and money, and why that makes climate action so politically difficult.

  • Politics of Gender

    Why gender inequality arises and why it lasts, from the U.S. Congress to Indian villages.

  • Comparative Political Economy

    How a country’s wealth gets produced and distributed — and whether governments spend it, share it, or steal it.

  • African Politics

    Why political and economic outcomes across Africa vary so much, between countries and over time.

  • Research and Writing (PS 290)

    Guides second-year PhD students through an independent research project, from first question to finished draft.

Prospective graduate students

I welcome inquiries from students interested in gender and political representation or political behavior or the politics of climate change — particularly those drawn to field-based and experimental research. Please review my published work before writing, and include a short note on the questions you want to pursue.
