Amanda Clayton

Associate Professor of Political Science
University of California, Berkeley
Vice-Chair and Director of Undergraduate Studies

I study the causes and consequences of women’s political representation — what draws women into politics, how electoral gender quotas reshape legislatures, and how citizens respond to who governs them. My recent work examines the gender politics of climate change — who resists decarbonization, and who is heard when environmental decisions get made.

About

Research

Much of my research is field-based and comparative, drawing on field and survey experiments, legislative debate records, and focus groups in Malawi, Uganda, Lesotho, Namibia, Kuwait, and the United States, alongside cross-national data covering more than a hundred countries. My current work on gender and climate politics includes a book project on the origins of gender differences in climate attitudes worldwide.

My work has appeared in leading academic journals including the American Political Science Review, American Journal of Political Science, Journal of Politics, and International Organization, and has been covered by The Economist, The New York Times, The Washington Post, and Teen Vogue, among others.

My research has been supported by the National Science Foundation, the Swedish Research Council, USAID, CGIAR, and EGAP, among others. More on coverage and support.

Empirical Study of Gender (EGEN) Research Network
